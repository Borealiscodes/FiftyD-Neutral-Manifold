🧪 Unofficial Bill Nye Tile Explainer: Stability Surfaces

> “Imagine drawing invisible fences inside a 50‑dimensional room.  
> They don’t protect anything.  
> They don’t mean anything.  
> They just mark where a math function crosses a line.”

Stability surfaces define neutral boundaries inside the manifold. They classify coordinates based on scalar field values — nothing more, nothing less.

No semantics.  
No governance.  
No runtime physics.  
Just math fences.

---

🧱 What a Stability Surface Is
A stability surface is:

- a scalar field  
- plus a threshold  
- producing a geometric boundary  

It’s the 50D equivalent of saying:

> “Everything with value ≥ T is inside the region.”

But without implying that “inside” or “outside” means anything.

---

🔢 How Stability Surfaces Work
Given:

- a coordinate  
- a scalar field  
- a threshold  

The surface:

- evaluates the coordinate  
- compares the value to the threshold  
- returns a classification  

This classification is purely geometric.

---

🧭 Why Stability Surfaces Exist
They provide:

- bounded regions  
- geometric partitions  
- neutral classification  
- simple demos for traversal and scalar fields  

They are the “math zones” of the manifold.

---

🎨 Visual Grammar (Allowed)
Stability surfaces may be illustrated using:

- contour lines  
- threshold curves  
- projection maps  
- abstract boundary shapes  

They may not be illustrated using:

- NDH altitude bands  
- governance lattices  
- runtime physics glyphs  
- semantic labels  
- narrative symbols  

This keeps the documentation neutral and educational.

---

🧩 How Stability Surfaces Interact with Other Modules
- Scalar Fields provide the values  
- Traversal Engine moves coordinates across boundaries  
- Operators transform coordinates before classification  
- Utilities support math operations  

Stability surfaces are the “region layer” of the repo.
